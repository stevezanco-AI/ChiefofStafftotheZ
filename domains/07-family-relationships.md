## Communication Style at Home

Steve's professional communication strengths — direct, conclusion-first, challenging — are assets at work and liabilities if brought home unmodified.

**Things that work at work but need adjustment at home:**
- "Leading with conclusion" can feel dismissive when someone needs to process out loud
- "Challenging thinking" can feel combative when someone wants to be heard, not improved
- "Direct communication" can come across as blunt when warmth is needed more than clarity

**What works at home:**
- Lead with curiosity before solutions: "Tell me more" before "Here's what I think you should do"
- Ask what kind of support is needed: "Do you want me to help solve this, or do you just need to vent?"
- Be slow to give advice and fast to listen
- Show appreciation specifically ("I noticed that you...") not generically

**Steve's self-check before hard conversations at home:** "Am I here to fix something or to be with this person?"

## Work-Life Integration

Steve doesn't separate work and life cleanly — few ambitious people do. The goal isn't perfect separation; it's intentional integration so that neither systematically loses.

**Integration principles:**
- Work hard during work hours so you can be fully present when work stops
- Communicate your schedule to family so they know what to expect — surprises are harder than known commitments
- When work bleeds into personal time by exception, acknowledge it and restore balance
- Don't bring work stress into the room without naming it: "I had a hard day — give me 20 minutes to decompress before I'm present"

**Transition ritual:**
The commute home (or the end of the workday for remote days) is a decompression opportunity. Something that marks the shift from work mode to home mode. Options:
- A walk or short workout
- 10 minutes of music before engaging at home
- Changing clothes as a physical signal of the transition
- A short voice memo summary of the day (gets it out of your head)

## When Work Bleeds Over

It will happen. The Q end push, the big customer crisis, the hire that needs to close. The goal isn't zero work bleeds — it's recognizing when they happen and resetting.

**Recognition signals:**
- Coming home distracted for more than 3 nights in a row
- Canceling or shortchanging personal commitments due to work more than once in a month
- Someone close to Steve comments on his absence or distraction

**Reset protocol:**
1. Acknowledge it plainly (to yourself and to the person): "I've been checked out. I know it."
2. Name what's driving it (is this a short-term crunch or a structural problem?)
3. Make a concrete plan to restore: a specific evening, outing, or conversation that re-establishes connection
4. If it's structural (work is always like this), that's a different conversation — see career-growth.md
