# Domain: Side Projects

**Domain tag:** `side-projects`
**Chief of Staff priority:** Medium — energizing and income-generating, but must never compromise the main role

---

## Overview

Steve runs two parallel side projects alongside his day job: Skatepark Respect (a community advocacy project) and consulting work (sales consulting for businesses). Both are intentional — not just hobbies. The challenge is ensuring they energize rather than drain him, and that neither competes with his primary career trajectory.

**The governing rule for side projects:**
If a side project starts taking time or mental energy from Steve's day job or his promotion path, it gets scaled back. The day job is the platform — everything else is built on top of it.

## Skatepark Respect

### What It Is

**Skatepark Respect, Inc.** — registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit (EIN# 82-5378615), founded January 2018, based in Oceanside, CA. Steve is Founder & Executive Director (8+ years running it as of 2026).

**Mission:** To create awareness and community involvement in keeping skateparks clean and maintained. The org conserves skateparks through outreach, cleanup efforts, and responsibility within the skateboarding community.

**Steve's President/ED duties:**
- Works with the Board of Directors on fiscal, operations, fundraising, marketing, and program strategy
- Provides leadership and decision-making processes for long- and short-term goals
- Builds and maintains a diverse, engaged Board
- Formulates marketing/branding/development strategy and enhances revenue from major donors, foundations, government agencies, and corporations
- Oversees financial status — long/short range financial plans, budget monitoring, financial controls
- Supports and motivates staff and volunteers
- Delivers programs through new and existing partnerships

**Core goals:**
- Advocate for better skatepark access and investment in the community
- Build connection and shared identity among local skaters across generations
- Counter negative perceptions of skate culture with direct community engagement
- Create programming or events that bring the community together
- Potentially influence city planning or parks departments on skatepark funding

### Why This Matters to Steve

This isn't just a hobby project — it's where Steve's leadership skills meet something he actually cares about. Community organizing builds exactly the cross-functional influence, stakeholder management, and communication skills that Director-level leadership requires. It just happens to be applied to skateparks instead of sales.

The credibility Steve builds here — knowing how to move a city council, work with diverse stakeholders, organize people around a cause — is real and transferable.

## Opportunities vs. Distractions

Steve gets asked to do things. Not all of them are worth doing. Use this filter:

**Take it if:**
- It directly advances Skatepark Respect goals OR generates consulting income
- The time investment is clear and bounded
- It energizes rather than depletes
- It builds skills or relationships useful to the promotion path
- It doesn't conflict with day job hours or obligations

**Decline if:**
- Timeline is vague and could expand
- The "opportunity" benefits the other party more than Steve
- It pulls focus from something already in progress
- Steve would say yes out of politeness, not enthusiasm
- It creates employer conflict or confidentiality risk

**The test:** If someone offered you this exact opportunity with 2 weeks less lead time, would you still want it? If no — it's not really a yes.
