## What Director-Level Actually Looks Like

Before pursuing the promotion, be clear on what it requires. Director of Sales typically means:

**Scope:**
- Manages multiple managers or a significantly larger team (not just 6-8 reps)
- Owns a larger P&L component or meaningful revenue line
- Sets strategy, not just executes it
- Involved in hiring, compensation planning, go-to-market decisions

**Behaviors:**
- Operates with less direction from above — takes initiative on problems before being asked
- Manages up proactively — boss rarely needs to check in
- Cross-functional by default — Finance, Product, Marketing are peers to engage, not obstacles
- Builds systems that survive the manager's absence
- Develops other managers, not just individual contributors

**Evidence needed:**
- Track record of team performance at or above quota
- Demonstrable rep development (people grew under Steve's leadership)
- Strategic contribution beyond current role scope
- Cross-functional influence wins
- Financial fluency (can speak to unit economics, not just revenue)

## Network Building for Promotion

The people who accelerate promotions are not always who you expect.

**Internal advocates to cultivate:**
- Your boss (obvious but critical — are they actively advocating?)
- Your boss's boss (do they know your name and your impact?)
- HR Business Partner (they're in the room when decisions get made)
- Peers from other functions who can speak to your cross-functional work

**External network for market intelligence and optionality:**
- Director/VP-level peers at other companies (benchmarking, perspective)
- Recruiters in your space (they know what the market values)
- Former manager or skip-level who moved on (honest feedback from a safe distance)
