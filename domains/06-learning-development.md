## Overview

Learning is how Steve closes the gap between where he is and where he's going. At Manager level, you can coast on existing skills for a while. At Director level, the landscape is different enough that you need to actively build new capabilities: financial fluency, executive communication, strategic thinking, cross-functional influence.

The failure mode here is consuming content without changing behavior. Books read, podcasts listened to, courses completed — none of it matters unless it shows up in how Steve leads and communicates.

## Key Concepts to Internalize

Things Steve should know cold — not just have read once.

**Sales leadership:**
- Pipeline coverage ratio target and what it means (3x minimum)
- How to build a forecast: stage-weighted vs. judgment-weighted
- The difference between coaching and managing (one builds skill, one monitors activity)
- How compensation plans drive behavior (and how to spot misaligned incentives)
- Why the best sales orgs have strict process AND people latitude

**Construction market:**
- Major economic indicators that drive commercial construction (interest rates, permits, GDP)
- How material cost changes flow through to contractor margins
- The difference between GC, subcontractor, and owner relationships
- How to read a Dodge/ENR market report

**Financial acumen:**
- EBITDA: what it is, why executives care about it
- Unit economics: cost to acquire a customer, lifetime value, payback period
- How to build a simple ROI model for a sales investment (headcount, tool, territory)
- What a healthy gross margin looks like in Steve's industry

## Knowledge Application

The most important question is not "what did I read?" but "what did I do differently because of it?"

**Steve's application rule:** Every book, course, or podcast should produce at least one of:
- A changed behavior (how I run 1:1s, how I structure my forecast, how I communicate upward)
- A new question I'm asking (in meetings, in reviews, of my team)
- A concrete experiment (trying something different for 30 days to see if it works)

If a piece of learning doesn't produce one of these, it was entertainment, not development.
