# Framework: Executive Narrative — Building Steve's Director-Level Brand

**Purpose:** The structured approach for building, documenting, and deploying the narrative that positions Steve for Director.

---

## Why This Exists

Promotions are not awarded for competence alone. They're awarded to people who are known and trusted at the level above them. The executive narrative is how Steve makes that happen — not by self-promoting, but by having clear, compelling, evidence-backed answers to the questions that matter in a promotion conversation.

This framework helps Steve:
1. Know what story he's telling about himself
2. Build the evidence base to back it up
3. Know where and how to deploy the narrative
4. Be ready for the promotion conversation when it comes

## Evidence Categories

A promotion case needs proof, not just claims. Build the evidence file continuously using /debrief.

### Category 1: Strategic Impact
Decisions and initiatives that moved the business — not just task completion.

Format for each entry:
```
Date: [Date]
Situation: [What was the business context?]
Decision/Initiative: [What did Steve do?]
Alternatives: [What else was considered?]
Outcome: [What happened? In dollar terms if possible]
```

## Where to Deploy the Narrative

The evidence and stories only create value when they're shared in the right settings.

### Performance Reviews
This is the primary deployment venue for the promotion case. Before any performance review:
- Pull 3-5 stories from the story bank
- Frame them in terms of impact at Director level, not just Manager level
- Lead with "I want to share evidence of operating beyond my current scope" — not just "here's what I did"

**Review strategy:**
- Come prepared with a one-pager on your impact (they may not have time to read it, but preparing it organizes your own thinking)
- Name the promotion explicitly: "I want to discuss my path to Director and where I stand against the criteria we discussed."

### Skip-Level Meetings
When Steve meets with his boss's boss, these conversations are visibility opportunities.

**What to do:**
- Ask one smart question about the business (shows strategic thinking)
- Share one piece of meaningful intel (shows market knowledge)
- If appropriate, briefly mention one recent win with context (shows impact)
- Do NOT give a 10-minute speech about your accomplishments — that's not how this works

**The goal:** Leave the skip-level thinking "Steve is sharp and thinking about the right things."

### Networking Conversations (External)
When meeting Director/VP peers at other companies or at events.

**How to position:**
- Lead with your expertise and perspective, not your title
- Share an insight about the construction market or sales leadership — something they'll find valuable
- The people who get remembered in networking are the ones who gave something, not the ones who positioned themselves

### Promotion Conversation (Direct)
When Steve explicitly asks for the promotion or has a formal conversation about it.

**Structure for the conversation:**
1. "I want to talk about my path to Director and where I stand." (Name it directly)
2. "Here's the evidence I've built against the criteria we discussed." (3-5 examples)
3. "What am I missing? What's between me and the decision?" (Ask for honesty)
4. "What does the next 90 days need to look like for this to move forward?" (Create a plan)

**Do not:**
- Wait to be offered the conversation
- Be vague about wanting the promotion
- Accept vague feedback without drilling into specifics
- Leave without a clear understanding of what happens next

## Common Questions for Director-Level Conversations

Build specific answers to these from the story bank.

**"What's your leadership philosophy?"**
Answer should be specific and owned — not generic ("I believe in empowering people"). What does Steve actually believe, based on what he's seen work?

**"Tell me about a time you turned around a struggling team or rep."**
Pull from Story Bank — Category: Team Development.

**"How do you handle conflict with a peer or stakeholder?"**
Answer should show maturity, directness, and a specific example.

**"What's your biggest leadership failure?"**
This question is about self-awareness and growth mindset. Steve should have a real answer that shows he learned something and changed.

**"What does Director-level look like versus where you are now?"**
This tests whether Steve understands the job he's asking for. He should be able to describe scope, behaviors, and expectations clearly — including the things he's still developing.

**"Why sales leadership vs. going back to individual contributor?"**
Steve's genuine answer. Why does he believe in developing people? What has he seen great leaders do?

**"What's the state of your market right now?"**
This is where construction market intel becomes a differentiator. Steve should be able to speak to this with depth and recency — not generic platitudes.

**"Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"**
Should be ambitious but grounded. The VP-level answer is about building something, not just climbing.

## Narrative Review Cadence

**Monthly:** Add new stories to the story bank from /debrief
**Quarterly:** Review and update evidence log. Is the narrative still the right one?
**Before any performance conversation or exec meeting:** Review 2-3 stories. Know them cold.
**Annually:** Full narrative review. Does this still reflect who Steve is and where he's going?
