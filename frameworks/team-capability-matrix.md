# Framework: Team Capability Matrix

**Purpose:** A structured tool for assessing sales rep capability, making development plans, and informing performance decisions.

---

## Overview

This matrix gives Steve a consistent, objective-ish lens for evaluating his team. The goal is not to reduce people to numbers, but to ensure that every rep has a clear and honest assessment — so development is targeted, performance decisions are defensible, and Steve doesn't accidentally treat high-potential underperformers the same as high-potential high-performers.

Run this assessment quarterly. Share elements of it (the development plan, not the scores) with each rep in their monthly check-in.

---

## Dimensions

### Dimension 1: Sales Results (What)
**Definition:** Actual quota performance and business outcomes delivered.

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Consistently 110%+ of quota. Pipeline always healthy. Win rate above team average. |
| 4 | Consistently 95–110%. Reliable. Occasionally above, rarely below. |
| 3 | 80–95%. Hit quota more often than not. Some patterns worth addressing. |
| 2 | 60–80% average. Inconsistent. Some quarters good, others concerning. |
| 1 | Below 60% consistently. Not hitting quota despite adequate tenure and support. |

---

### Dimension 2: Sales Skill (How — Technical)
**Definition:** Command of sales process, methodology, and craft. Can they run a deal well?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Executes every stage exceptionally. Discovery is deep. Demos are tailored. Objections handled fluently. Can close complex deals independently. |
| 4 | Strong in most stages. One identifiable gap. Handles most deals well with minimal coaching. |
| 3 | Competent in basics. Needs coaching on 2-3 specific skills. Occasionally gets stuck. |
| 2 | Gaps in multiple stages. Needs frequent coaching. Struggles with complex or non-standard deals. |
| 1 | Fundamental skill gaps. Cannot execute the sales process without significant support. |

---

### Dimension 3: Attitude & Coachability (How — Behavioral)
**Definition:** Mindset, receptivity to feedback, effort, and professional conduct.

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Actively seeks feedback. Applies coaching immediately and consistently. Positive contributor to team culture. Goes above required effort. |
| 4 | Receptive to feedback. Usually applies it. Good attitude. Pulls weight on the team. |
| 3 | Accepts feedback but application is inconsistent. Generally positive but doesn't contribute beyond own results. |
| 2 | Defensive about feedback. Applies it sporadically. Neutral to negative impact on team culture. |
| 1 | Resists or argues with feedback. May undermine team culture. Low discretionary effort. |

---

### Dimension 4: Growth Trajectory (Future)
**Definition:** Rate of improvement over the past 6-12 months. Are they getting better?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Demonstrably better in multiple dimensions vs. 6 months ago. On a steep growth curve. |
| 4 | Clear improvement in target areas. Moving in the right direction. |
| 3 | Some improvement. Progress slower than expected but positive trend. |
| 2 | Flat. Not getting better despite coaching. |
| 1 | Declining. Performance or behaviors are regressing. |

---

### Dimension 5: Industry/Domain Knowledge (Edge)
**Definition:** Depth of knowledge about construction, the customer's business, and market dynamics.

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 5 | Customers seek their perspective. Deep understanding of their world. Can lead conversations as a peer. |
| 4 | Strong domain knowledge. Credible in most customer conversations. |
| 3 | Adequate. Can have basic conversations. Some gaps customers notice. |
| 2 | Limited domain knowledge. Customers can tell. |
| 1 | Minimal knowledge. Hurts credibility in customer conversations. |

### Q1: High Performer (High Results + High Attitude)
**Who is here:** Your best reps. High results AND coachable and positive.

**What they need:**
- Recognition and visibility — they should feel valued
- Stretch assignments — give them harder deals, leadership opportunities
- Career conversations — where do they want to go? Are they a future team lead?
- Retention focus — know what would make them leave and address it

**Conversation guide:**
"You're performing at a high level and I want to make sure you're growing, not just executing. What would make the next 6 months feel like a step forward for you personally?"

**Risk:** Neglect. These reps often get less attention because they don't create problems. That's a mistake — they have the most options if they decide to leave.

---

### Q2: Star (High Results + Low/Variable Attitude)
**Who is here:** Reps who hit their number but are difficult — negative attitude, resistant to feedback, bad for team culture.

**What they need:**
- Direct feedback on the attitude/behavior impact (not the results)
- Understanding of what's driving the negativity (sometimes it's a fixable problem)
- Clear expectation: results are table stakes, behavior is also expected
- A decision: can this be fixed, or is this a person/environment mismatch?

**Conversation guide:**
"I want to talk about something separate from your results, which are strong. The [specific behavior] is creating [specific impact] on the team. I need this to change. Here's specifically what I need to see differently..."

**Risk:** Tolerance. Many managers let attitude slide because results are there. The cost is team culture and other reps' morale. High performers who don't act like team members are net negative past a certain point.

**Hard truth:** A rep who consistently poisons the team's culture isn't a star — they're expensive regardless of their quota.

---

### Q3: At-Risk (Low Results + Low Attitude)
**Who is here:** Reps who aren't performing AND aren't showing the coachability or attitude that would suggest improvement is coming.

**What they need:**
- Honest conversation: "Here's where we are. Here's what needs to change. Here's the timeline."
- A formal improvement plan with specific metrics
- Steve's documentation and records in order

**Conversation guide:**
"I need to be honest with you. Your results and your engagement have both been below where they need to be, and I'm not seeing the improvement curve I need to see. I want to give you a clear picture of what has to change and the timeline. If we can get there, great — I want you to succeed. But I also need to be straight with you about where we are."

**Risk:** Delay. Managers let Q3 reps linger for months out of discomfort. The cost is team morale, quota performance, and eventually the manager's credibility. Address it within 60 days.

**Process:**
1. Coaching plan (documented, specific, 30 days)
2. If no improvement: formal PIP with HR (clear metrics, timeline, consequences)
3. If PIP not met: exit process

---

### Q4: Developing (Low Results + High Attitude)
**Who is here:** Reps who are working hard and receptive to coaching, but haven't turned it into results yet.

**What they need:**
- Diagnosis of the specific gap (Is it skill? Market knowledge? Deal complexity? Ramp time?)
- Targeted coaching on the specific barrier
- A timeline: how long is a reasonable ramp to results given the context?
- Encouragement that's honest (not false positivity)

**Conversation guide:**
"I see the effort and the coachability — that matters and I notice it. Here's what I'm seeing as the specific gap that's affecting your results. Here's what I want to focus on with you in the next 30 days. I believe you can close this gap — here's how we're going to work on it together."

**Risk:** Over-patience. Developing reps can plateau in Q4 — high attitude, insufficient results — and the coaching never connects. Watch the growth trajectory score. If it's flat after 2-3 quarters of coaching, the diagnosis is wrong or the role is wrong.

**Rule of thumb:** Q4 reps in their first 6 months = investment. Q4 reps beyond 12 months = a conversation about fit.

## Development Planning

For each rep, maintain a development plan with:

1. **Current quadrant:** Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4
2. **Target quadrant:** (Usually Q1 or movement toward it)
3. **Primary development focus:** The ONE dimension that would have the most impact
4. **Specific behaviors to change:** Not "improve attitude" — "When Steve gives feedback in 1:1s, [Rep] listens without interrupting and implements at least one suggestion before the next meeting"
5. **Support Steve is providing:** What coaching specifically
6. **Check-in cadence:** When will Steve assess progress?
7. **Timeline to next review:** 30/60/90 days

**Development plan template per rep:**

```
Rep: [Name]
As of: [Date]
Current quadrant: [Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4]

Assessment summary:
- Results: [Score]/5 — [One sentence]
- Skill: [Score]/5 — [One sentence]
- Attitude: [Score]/5 — [One sentence]
- Growth trajectory: [Score]/5 — [One sentence]
- Domain knowledge: [Score]/5 — [One sentence]

Primary development focus: [The one thing]
Specific target behaviors:
  - [Behavior 1 — observable and specific]
  - [Behavior 2 — observable and specific]

What Steve is doing:
  - [Coaching action 1]
  - [Coaching action 2]

30-day milestone: [What should be different in 30 days?]
90-day milestone: [What should be different in 90 days?]

Retention risk: Green / Yellow / Red
What would make them leave: [Honest assessment]
Mitigation: [What Steve is doing]
```

## Retention Risk Signals

Watch for these before the rep starts interviewing:

**Early warning signals:**
- Decrease in proactive communication (stops sharing wins, goes quieter)
- Less engagement in team meetings
- Questions about compensation or comp plan that come out of nowhere
- LinkedIn profile updated recently (check quietly)
- Subtle distancing from team relationships

**Yellow signals:**
- Missed quota for 2+ quarters AND has been a strong performer historically
- Internal frustration they've expressed but hasn't been resolved
- Their peer got promoted and they didn't

**Red signals:**
- Has told Steve they're thinking about leaving
- Is visibly checking out
- Has been approached by a competitor (they may or may not tell you)

**Retention conversation:**
Don't wait for the exit interview. Have the conversation when you see yellow signals. "I want to check in on how you're feeling about the role and what would make the next year here feel like it's worth it for you."

## How to Use This Matrix

**Quarterly cadence:**
1. Score every rep before the quarterly review meeting
2. Identify movement from last quarter (is anyone improving? declining?)
3. Update development plans
4. Calibrate with HR on anyone in Q3 or any formal process

**In 1:1s:**
- Share relevant parts of the assessment (not the scores — the substance)
- The development focus should be explicit, not implied
- Rep should know which quadrant they're in, in plain language

**For promotion decisions (rep to lead):**
- Q1 consistently for 3+ quarters
- Growth trajectory score of 4-5
- Domain knowledge strong enough to mentor others
- Attitude/coachability that models the team culture Steve is building

**For PIP decisions:**
- Q3 consistently (not one bad quarter)
- Direct coaching conversation hasn't produced change
- HR involved from the start — document everything

*The team is Steve's most important output. How well he develops and manages them is the single strongest piece of evidence for Director-level leadership.*
