# Mistakes & Lessons Log

**Purpose:** This log captures real mistakes — decisions that didn't land, conversations that went wrong, things Steve should have done differently. The goal is not self-criticism. It's compound learning. Every mistake logged here becomes a pattern to recognize next time.

The leaders Steve admires most are not the ones who made the fewest mistakes. They're the ones who made mistakes once, learned from them fast, and never made the same one twice.

---

## Why This Matters

Steve's values include accountability and continuous improvement. Those values are meaningless without a mechanism for capturing and applying what went wrong. This log is that mechanism.

The Chief of Staff will reference this file when relevant patterns emerge. If Steve logs a mistake about rushing into a conversation unprepared, and then a similar high-stakes conversation is coming up, the Chief of Staff will flag it: "Last time you had a conversation like this, here's what didn't go well."

---

## How to Log a Mistake

After any significant miss — a conversation that went sideways, a decision that backfired, an opportunity missed — run /debrief and answer:

1. What happened? (Factual, not self-flagellating)
2. What did I do or not do that contributed to the outcome?
3. What should I have done instead?
4. What's the lesson? (One clear sentence)
5. How will I apply this? (Specific — the next time X situation arises, I will do Y)

---

## Entry Format

```
## [Date] — [Short title that captures the mistake]

**Situation:** [What was the context? What was at stake?]

**What happened:** [Factual account of the mistake or miss]

**What I contributed:** [Honest self-assessment — what did Steve do or not do?]

**What I should have done:** [Specific alternative action]

**The lesson:** [One clear sentence — start with "When..." or "Before..." or "Always..."]

**How I'll apply it:** [The next time [specific trigger], I will [specific action]]

**Status:** Open (not yet tested) / Tested (applied and worked) / Integrated (this is now default behavior)
```

## Patterns to Watch For

As entries accumulate, the Chief of Staff will look for patterns:

- **Preparation failures:** Mistakes that could have been prevented with more or better preparation
- **Communication timing:** Waiting too long to have a conversation
- **Overconfidence:** Moving fast on assumptions that turned out to be wrong
- **Underconfidence:** Hesitating when action was clearly called for
- **Relationship neglect:** Situations where a stronger relationship would have changed the outcome
- **Scope creep:** Taking on more than could be executed well

---

## Annual Mistakes Review

Once per year, read every entry and ask:

1. What are the patterns? Where did I make the same type of mistake more than once?
2. Which mistakes were genuinely unforeseeable vs. which were foreseeable with better process?
3. What's the one mistake I'm most glad I won't make again?
4. What mistake am I probably still making that I haven't recognized yet?
